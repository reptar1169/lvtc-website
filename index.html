Current business

What the council is working on

Accessory dwelling units are the big one. Members have brought the same complaint to meeting after meeting: four, six and nine-unit ADU complexes going up between single-family houses, with no parking added and no traffic or infrastructure work. The City reformed the rules in 2025. We do not think it went far enough — what we asked for, and what came back →

Every relaxation of ADU rules has been justified by the housing crisis. This is that crisis, measured: the median price of an existing single-family home in San Diego County, with the two dates the rules were loosened marked on it.

Also in front of us

Three other things

Each one is somewhere the council can actually move the City, and each one is stronger with more residents behind it.

Active

Speeding on Comstock Street

The limit is 25 mph and it is not being enforced. After a crash that totalled three cars, we surveyed the neighborhood: 49 residents told us what they want done.

Watching

Rapid Route 41 bus lane

SANDAG's Next Gen Rapid Route 41 would run Hillcrest to UC San Diego through Linda Vista. Planning was completed in 2023 and SANDAG is still securing funding — which is the window to weigh in.

Polling now

Beach parking, and the county sales tax

An ordinance to keep beach and Mission Bay parking free is waiting on a City Council vote, and a half-cent county sales tax is on the November ballot. Both are still open. We want a neighborhood position before they are decided.

Beyond Linda Vista

Balboa Park is getting a new master plan

It isn’t in Linda Vista, but it’s a short drive away and plenty of us use it. The City is rewriting the plan that decides what the park becomes over the next few decades, and it is asking the public first.

City survey · open now

Tell the City what you want from the park

About five to ten minutes, anonymous. It asks which parts of the park you use, and has you rank what should come first — restrooms, shade and seating; playgrounds; gardens and trails; historic buildings; dining; and safer, better-lit walkways and bridges. There is room at the end for your own ideas.

This is the City’s survey, not ours: answers go straight to the City Planning Department. It’s the first round — workshops come later, and the finished plan is due in 2028. Questions go to planbalboapark@sandiego.gov.
