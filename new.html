Welcome to Linda Vista

Everything below is the kind of thing that takes about a year to work out on your own. Every link goes to the City or the organization itself, so it stays right even when we forget to update this page.

The first week

Four things to sort out before anything else.

Find your trash day

The City collects from single-family homes and small buildings on a set weekday that varies block to block. Put in your address and it tells you the day, plus which weeks are recycling and green waste.

Collection schedule lookup →

Report a problem to the City

Potholes, broken streetlights, graffiti, illegal dumping, an abandoned vehicle, a missed pickup. Get It Done is the City's reporting system and it works on the web or as a phone app. Reports get a tracking number, which matters if you have to follow up.

Get It Done →

You're in Council District 7

Linda Vista is one of eight communities in District 7, along with Mission Valley, Serra Mesa, Tierrasanta, Grantville, Allied Gardens, Del Cerro and San Carlos. Your councilmember is Raul Campillo. The office line is 619-236-6677.

Places worth knowing

Hours change. Call ahead if you are making a trip for something specific.

Linda Vista Library

2160 Ulric St
858-573-1399

Mon–Tue 11:30am–8pm · Wed–Sat 9:30am–6pm · closed Sunday. Public computers, Wi-Fi, a meeting room, and a collection with a lot of Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese and Japanese titles. A library card is free for anyone who lives, works or goes to school in California.

Linda Vista Recreation Center

7064 Levant St
858-573-1392

Gym, auditorium, skate park, four tennis courts, two softball fields, a tot lot and a basketball court. The meeting rooms can be rented. Hours are roughly Mon–Thu 10am–8pm, Fri 11am–7pm, weekends 9am–3pm, and the center closes on City holidays.

Rec center page →

Tecolote Canyon Natural Park

Nature Center · 5180 Tecolote Rd
858-581-9944

About 6.5 miles of trail for walking, running and mountain biking, running north–south between Linda Vista and Clairemont. The Nature Center is open Tuesday through Saturday, 10am–4pm.

Bayside Community Center

2202 Comstock St

A neighborhood institution: food distribution, youth and senior programs, immigration and citizenship help, and a lot of the organizing muscle behind the Multicultural Fair. If you want to volunteer somewhere in Linda Vista, start here.

Farmers Market

6931 Linda Vista Rd

Every Thursday, 2–7pm, year round. EBT and WIC are accepted. It is also the single easiest place to meet your neighbors without having to make an occasion of it.

Skateworld

6907 Linda Vista Rd
858-560-9349

A roller rink that has been on Linda Vista Road since 1975, in a building the rink's own history says was dedicated by Eleanor Roosevelt. Family night, adult sessions, a sensory-friendly session once a month. Closed Mondays; check the schedule before you go, it varies by day.

Who decides what

This is the part nobody explains, and it is why people show up to the wrong meeting and leave frustrated.

Linda Vista Town Council

Us · the neighborhood's voice, no legal authority

We have no legal authority over anything. What we have is a room, a mailing list and a record: we collect what neighbors actually think, put it in writing, and hand it to the people who can act. Speeding on Comstock and the Rapid 41 bus lane are current examples. Anyone who lives or works in Linda Vista can attend and speak; membership is only needed to vote.

Our meetings and minutes →

Linda Vista Community Planning Group

Land use · buildings, zoning, development

A separate, City-recognized advisory board that reviews development proposals and makes formal recommendations to the City. If your issue is a project going up down the street, this is the meeting you want, not ours. They meet the 4th Monday of the month at 6pm, currently at the Kearny Mesa Recreation Center.

Planning group page →

Councilmember Raul Campillo, District 7

Actual power · budget, ordinances, City departments

Your elected representative on the City Council. The office is who you escalate to when a Get It Done report goes nowhere, and who votes on the money. A single email from one resident carries less weight than the same email backed by a room full of them — which is the entire reason this council exists.

District 7 office →

Assemblymember Chris Ward, District 78

State law · the rules the City has to work inside

Some of what people bring to us is not the City's to fix. The ADU rules are the clearest case: Sacramento set the frame, and San Diego built its program inside it. If you want that frame changed, this is the level where it happens. Ward's office takes proposals straight from residents through a bill idea form — it asks for your name, your address and the idea, and it is open to constituents of Assembly District 78. Linda Vista has been split between Assembly districts in the past, so if you live toward the western edge it is worth confirming which district you are in first.

Send him a bill idea →

Short version A pothole is Get It Done. A new apartment building is the Planning Group. A city policy or a budget line is Councilmember Campillo. A state law — the ADU rules, for instance — is Assemblymember Ward. Anything where it helps to have a hundred neighbors saying the same thing at once is us.

Every spring, Linda Vista Road closes for a street fair that has been running for four decades. It is the neighborhood at its best and the single clearest answer to the question of what Linda Vista is.

Why the neighborhood looks like this

The curving streets, the small single-story houses sitting close together, the canyon nobody ever built on — none of that is accident. Linda Vista was built all at once, in 1941: three thousand homes in 259 days, because the aircraft plants needed somewhere to put their workers. It was the largest defense housing project in the country. Everything here since has been built on top of that decision, and you can still see it from the sidewalk.

How Linda Vista got here

The fastest way in

Come to one general meeting. You do not have to join, speak, or know anything first — people mingle from 6:00 and the agenda starts at 6:30. If you'd rather start smaller, the poll on the home page takes about thirty seconds and the results go straight into what we bring to the City.
