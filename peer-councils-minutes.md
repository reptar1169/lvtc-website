# Do other San Diego town councils post their minutes?

Checked 21 September 2026. Thirteen neighborhood groups, looking only at what a
resident can find on the group's own public website.

**Of the twelve that could be assessed: five publish a working minutes archive,
one posts agendas only, five publish nothing, and one is a dormant archive of a
dissolved organization.**

Only **Tierrasanta** has what you'd call a complete public record — draft minutes
for essentially every meeting, organized by year, going back fifteen years.
Scripps Ranch has a deeper archive than LVTC but worse holes in it: **no minutes
at all for 2024**, and a roughly two-year gap between October 2023 and October
2025. Allied Gardens is current to within a month but keeps only about three
documents up at a time, so there is no archive to speak of.

**Where LVTC sits.** 40 of 75 due meetings posted, ~53%, continuous back to
January 2023 with the gaps visible and labeled. That is better than five councils
that post nothing, better than Serra Mesa, better than La Jolla's
request-only policy, and better than Allied Gardens on depth. Behind Tierrasanta
on completeness. Roughly comparable to Scripps Ranch, and arguably ahead of it,
since LVTC's gaps are disclosed on the page rather than left for the reader to
discover.

## The comparison that actually matters: Linda Vista's own Planning Group

The **Linda Vista Community Planning Group** publishes a continuous, dated,
navigable minutes archive at lindavistaplanning.org — most recent posted minutes
June 22, 2026, unbroken monthly coverage verified back to November 2024 and
continuing further, with skipped months labeled as adjournments rather than left
blank.

They do that because they have to. City Council Policy 600-24 requires
recognized planning groups to comply with the Brown Act, which mandates posting
agendas 72 hours ahead; the policy separately requires them to retain minutes
for **five years** and produce them to any member of the public on request. The
City also mirrors the records on sandiego.gov.

One nuance worth having right: **posting minutes to a website is "encouraged,"
not mandated, even for planning groups.** The mandate is retention and
production on request. LVPG goes beyond what's required — and so, in its own
way, does LVTC.

## What this means for the board's decision

The two options in the email look different in light of this.

**Option 1 (amend the bylaws to require posting)** would put LVTC alongside
Tierrasanta as one of the few councils in the city with a real public record,
and would match what the Planning Group already does in the same neighborhood.

**Option 2 (take the page down)** is not unusual — five of twelve peers publish
nothing. But it would move LVTC from the better half of the peer set into the
group with Clairemont, Ocean Beach, Kensington-Talmadge and Normal Heights. And
it would sit awkwardly beside the Planning Group's full archive, in a
neighborhood where the council's own website explains to new residents what the
difference between the two bodies is.

## Confidence and limits

- **Pacific Beach and Mission Beach are undetermined.** PB's minutes page blocks
  automated fetching; search-indexed files run to Feb 2022 but later ones may
  exist. Mission Beach hosts minutes in Dropbox folders that can't be listed
  automatically. Both publish *something*; neither could be dated.
- **Rancho Bernardo could not be reached at all** — its certificate doesn't match
  its hostname. No claim either way.
- Earlier-year depth for Tierrasanta and Scripps Ranch was sampled, not audited
  page by page.
- Bylaws PDFs for most peers weren't read line by line, so an internal posting
  obligation buried in one can't be ruled out.

Anything here is worth a spot-check before it goes in front of the board — the
Tierrasanta and Scripps Ranch pages are the two most useful to look at yourself.
